# Phase 15 — 2026-05-13 (engineering-craft seeds vs computing-history)

Fifteenth real-world pass. **Does the content of injected dreams
matter, or just the wrapper?** Phase 14 nailed down the headline:
imagination injection wins ~28% of head-to-heads on technical
prompts (CI [21%, 36%]). Phase 15 asks: would the win rate improve
if the dream archive contained engineering-shaped imagery instead
of historical-figure imagery?

## TL;DR

* **Engineering-craft seeds beat computing-history seeds 44/70
  (62.9%)** on technical-debugging prompts.
* **95% Wilson CI: [51.1%, 73.2%]** — lower bound just above 50%,
  so the effect is statistically significant (one-sided
  α=0.05).
* **Two scenarios are dramatic wins at 90% EC-win-rate:**
  retry-under-load and legacy-deletion. Both have a "system holds
  hidden context over years" shape that the new seeds (the retry
  policy that worked for three years until..., the feature flag
  meant to be temporary in 2017) map directly into.
* Cost: $0.40 across 210 API calls in 94 seconds.
* **The shipping engineering preference order (engineering-craft
  FIRST, computing-history second) is justified by data.**

## What shipped this phase

A new ``engineering-craft`` seed category in
``bog_agents_cli/dreamscape/seeds.py`` — 15 hand-curated entries.
The texture differs from ``computing-history``: where that category
features historical figures (Hamilton at Apollo, Engelbart's 1968
demo, Hopper's moth, Knuth interrupting TAOCP), the new category
features the day-to-day craft of long-lived software:

* *"The 3am page where the on-call engineer found the bug had been
  live for six years."*
* *"The git bisect that ended at the merge commit whose author had
  left the company."*
* *"The retry policy that worked perfectly for three years, until
  the downstream API added a 429 response code."*
* *"The feature flag that was meant to be temporary in 2017 and is
  now load-bearing."*
* *"The TODO comment from 2014 that was wrong then and is wrong
  now in the exact opposite way."*

In ``bog_agents_cli/dreamscape/domain.py``, the engineering
preference order now reads:
``engineering-craft → computing-history → history → space``.
Research domain also gets engineering-craft as a secondary
preference (research is craft-adjacent).

Two new unit tests pin the library shape and the preference order
so future edits can't regress them.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| retry-under-load | 9 | 1 | 0 | **90%** | [60%, 98%] |
| legacy-deletion | 9 | 1 | 0 | **90%** | [60%, 98%] |
| refactor-decision | 7 | 3 | 0 | 70% | [40%, 89%] |
| permission-denied-chmod | 6 | 4 | 0 | 60% | [31%, 83%] |
| flaky-test-ci-only | 5 | 4 | 1 | 50% | [24%, 76%] |
| slow-prod-query | 5 | 4 | 1 | 50% | [24%, 76%] |
| python-memory-creep | 3 | 6 | 1 | 30% | [11%, 60%] |

The wins concentrate where the engineering-craft texture **maps
directly** to the prompt. The two 90% wins are exactly the
scenarios about long-lived systems with hidden context. The lone
CH win (memory-creep at 30%) is a scenario about a *specific
in-the-moment debug* — perhaps historical-figure framing serves
better there because it offers a different lens.

The middle scenarios (flaky-test, slow-prod-query) are ties — both
seed sets help equally.

## What this means combined with Phase 14

Phase 14 showed imagination injection wins ~28% of head-to-heads
against control on technical prompts. Phase 15 shows **engineering-
craft seeds beat computing-history seeds ~63% of the time** when
the underlying technique is the same.

We didn't directly measure engineering-craft-vs-no-injection — that
would need a future Phase 16 combined experiment. But the
implication is reasonable: if EC seeds are clearly better than CH
seeds, then a hypothetical EC-vs-control measurement would land
somewhere *above* Phase 14's 28% — possibly in the 35-45% range. Still
likely below 50% (so imagination injection by default would still
hurt average technical-prompt quality), but materially better than
the historical-figure default Phase 14 measured.

The scenarios where EC wins big (retry-under-load 90%, legacy-deletion
90%) hint that **per-scenario routing** could push the win rate
higher still: those two scenarios alone might cross 50% vs no-
injection control with EC seeds. The other five are too noisy to
say.

## The methodological note

Phase 15 didn't include a no-injection control arm. That keeps the
cost low ($0.40 vs Phase 14's $2.10) and answers the specific
question I asked ("which seeds win?"). The trade-off is we can't
directly claim "engineering-craft makes imagination-injection
net-positive on technical prompts" — only "engineering-craft beats
computing-history within the imagination-injection regime."

A future Phase 16 (suggested) would re-run the same scenarios with
three arms: control, treatment-with-engineering-craft,
treatment-with-computing-history. That would directly answer the
combined question.

## Verdict

**WIN** — content matters. Engineering agents should dream of
engineering-craft, not historical figures in computing. The shipped
preference order (engineering-craft first) is justified by Phase 15
data.

## Next steps

* **Phase 16** — combined three-arm experiment on technical prompts:
  control vs treatment-with-EC-seeds vs treatment-with-CH-seeds.
  Directly measures whether engineering-craft seeds close enough of
  Phase 14's 28% gap to make imagination injection net-positive
  for engineering work.
* **Phase 17** — per-prompt routing. The 90% wins on
  retry-under-load and legacy-deletion vs 30% on memory-creep
  suggests prompts have shape-level affinity for specific seed
  styles. Classify the prompt, not just the agent.
* **Phase 18** — expand the engineering-craft library toward 30+
  entries. At 15 entries, an engineer who dreams once an hour for
  a day would see most seeds twice.
