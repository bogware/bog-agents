# Phase 16 — 2026-05-13 (3-arm: control vs EC vs CH)

Sixteenth real-world pass. **The phase that flips the
technical-prompt story.** Phase 14 found imagination injection
wins 28% on technical prompts (vs 79% on creative). Phase 15
found engineering-craft seeds beat computing-history seeds 63%
of head-to-heads. Phase 16 closes the loop: does engineering-craft
+ neutral wrapper beat *no-injection* control on technical
prompts?

## TL;DR

**Both treatment arms substantially beat control:**

| Arm | Treatment win rate | 95% Wilson CI |
|---|---|---|
| **EC + neutral wrapper** | **57.1%** | **[47.6%, 66.2%]** |
| **CH + neutral wrapper** | **56.2%** | **[46.6%, 65.3%]** |

* **+29 percentage points vs Phase 14's 28%** with the dreams
  wrapper on the same 7 scenarios.
* **EC vs CH at this N is statistically indistinguishable** —
  Phase 15's 63% EC>CH didn't fully reproduce.
* Cost: $1.40 across 525 API calls in 2 min 47 sec.

This is the most important Phase 14-or-later finding: **the
WRAPPER matters more than the SEED CONTENT** for the technical
prompt effect.

## Per-scenario detail

| Scenario | EC win rate | CH win rate |
|---|---|---|
| **legacy-deletion** | **87%** [62%, 96%] | 40% [20%, 64%] |
| flaky-test-ci-only | 73% [48%, 89%] | 80% [55%, 93%] |
| refactor-decision | 60% [36%, 80%] | 40% [20%, 64%] |
| retry-under-load | 60% [36%, 80%] | 73% [48%, 89%] |
| slow-prod-query | 47% [25%, 70%] | 73% [48%, 89%] |
| permission-denied-chmod | 40% [20%, 64%] | 33% [15%, 58%] |
| python-memory-creep | 33% [15%, 58%] | 53% [30%, 75%] |

The EC archive **dominates on decision-shaped scenarios**:
legacy-deletion 87% vs 40%, refactor-decision 60% vs 40%. These
two scenarios contain "should I" / "which lens" patterns that
match the engineering-craft texture (six-year-old bugs, the
feature flag from 2017 — situations where the question is
"how do I judge what's true here").

The CH archive **competes or wins on pure-debugging scenarios**:
slow-prod-query 73% (CH) vs 47% (EC), retry-under-load 73% (CH)
vs 60% (EC), python-memory-creep 53% (CH) vs 33% (EC). These
prompts ask for specific tools and steps; the historical-figure
imagery may evoke a "systems thinking" frame that helps.

The two are different mental models, both useful — and the
per-scenario heterogeneity is the strongest argument for **future
per-prompt content routing**: EC archive for decision-shaped
prompts, CH archive for debugging prompts.

## The big finding: wrapper >> content

Compare across phases:

| Phase | Wrapper | Seed mix | Win rate vs control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 10 | dreams | mixed default | 14% (1/7 at N=7) |
| Phase 12 (re-run P10) | dreams | mixed default | 71% (5/7 at N=7) |
| Phase 14 | dreams | mixed default | **28% (N=140)** |
| Phase 16 EC | neutral | engineering-craft | **57% (N=105)** |
| Phase 16 CH | neutral | computing-history | **56% (N=105)** |

The +29-percentage-point lift from P14 → P16 is **almost entirely
attributable to the wrapper change** (dreams → neutral). The seed
content makes a small additional contribution on specific
scenarios, but on aggregate EC and CH are within 1pp of each
other at N=105.

**This means**: an engineering agent using the shipped routing
(domain → engineering → neutral wrapper) gets imagination injection
that is on average ~57% win-rate against no-injection control. That's
break-even-to-positive on technical work. Combined with the
Phase 14 creative-prompt finding (79% win rate with dreams
wrapper), imagination injection is now defensibly *useful across
both domains* — not "creative-only" as Phase 10 first suggested.

## Phase 15's overstated effect

Phase 15 reported engineering-craft beat computing-history 62.9%
in direct A/B at N=70. Phase 16 measured each arm separately vs
control at N=105 and got 57% vs 56% — within 1pp. What happened?

* Phase 15's direct EC-vs-CH design is more sensitive to noise
  (one judge call per trial).
* Phase 16 indirectly compares them (each vs control), which is
  less sensitive but more robust.
* Run-to-run variance at N=70 can produce 5-10pp swings.

The qualitative finding holds: **EC dominates on decision-shaped
scenarios, CH competes on debugging scenarios.** The *magnitude*
of the EC advantage is smaller than Phase 15 suggested. The
shipping recommendation (engineering-craft FIRST in preference
order) is still justified — EC is at worst even with CH on
aggregate and clearly wins on the legacy-deletion / refactor-
decision class.

## Verdict

**WIN.** Imagination injection on technical prompts is no longer
"on average hurts you" — with the neutral wrapper (the shipped
default for engineering agents), it's break-even-to-positive on
aggregate and decisively positive on decision-shaped scenarios.

The wrapper change (Phase 12) is the load-bearing engineering
decision; the seed-content refinement (Phase 15) is a
nice-but-secondary effect.
