# Phase 26 — 2026-05-13 (N=30 confirmation of P21 content routing)

Twenty-sixth real-world pass. Phase 21 reported 47.6% routed-win
rate with 95% CI [38%, 57%] at N=15 per scenario — wide enough to
straddle 50%. Phase 26 doubles N to 30 per scenario (210 total
trials) to halve the CI width and settle whether per-prompt content
routing is truly neutral or whether the slight negative lean was
real.

## TL;DR

**Per-prompt content routing (keyword classifier) actively
HURTS outcomes.**

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Routed wins | 84/210 (**40.0%**) |
| Static wins | 115/210 |
| Ties | 11/210 |
| Wilson 95% CI on routed | **[33.6%, 46.7%]** |

**Lower CI bound is 33.6% — entirely below 50%.** Static routing
wins by ~10 percentage points. Phase 21's 47.6% was at the
optimistic edge of the noise band; the true rate at N=210 is ~40%.

## Per-kind breakdown

| Kind | Routed | Static | Routed rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| debugging | 35 | 47 (+8 ties) | **39%** | [30%, 49%] |
| decision | 20 | 39 (+1 tie) | **33%** | [23%, 46%] |
| creative | 29 | 29 (+2 ties) | **48%** | [36%, 61%] |

* **Debugging:** routed loses 39% vs static 52%. Even on
  scenarios where the keyword classifier *correctly* picks EC
  seeds (matching the debugging shape), the static arm's mixed
  sampling wins.
* **Decision:** routed at 33% is the worst case. The keyword
  classifier flips these to creative (via decision-pattern
  detection) → myth seeds, but an engineering-styled agent doesn't
  benefit from myth-only injection.
* **Creative:** ~tied at 48% [36%, 61%]. Routed → myth-only;
  static → mixed. The two are roughly equivalent on creative
  prompts.

## The big lesson

Phase 26 reveals that **the issue isn't classifier accuracy — it's
filtering itself reducing diversity.** Even on debugging prompts
where keyword classification is correct ("EC seeds for engineering
prompt"), the routed arm loses to the static arm's mixed sampling.
Filtering down to pure-EC removes the cross-domain variety that
mixed sampling inadvertently provides.

This was visible as a hint in Phase 21 (slow-prod-query at 67%
routed, but other scenarios mostly tied or routed-losing). At
N=210 the pattern resolves into a clear ~10pp deficit.

## Phase 21 → Phase 26 comparison

| Phase | N | Routed win rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| P21 | 105 (15×7) | 47.6% | [38.3%, 57.1%] |
| **P26** | **210 (30×7)** | **40.0%** | **[33.6%, 46.7%]** |

The point estimate shifted DOWN (toward worse) as the CI tightened.
P21 was the optimistic edge of the noise band; P26's tighter
measurement shows the truth lives at ~40%.

## What this means for shipping

`ImaginationConfig.use_content_routing` defaults to False. **Keep
it that way.** The Phase 21 mechanism is preserved as a knob for
future research, but the data now says enabling it actively
degrades response quality on the modal stuck-developer prompt.

The shipped agent-level routing from Phase 11/12 (validated at
N=140 in P14, N=60 in P20 on decision-shaped) is the load-bearing
decision. Per-prompt routing — both wrapper (P17) and content
(P21/P26) — does not pay off in measurable judge preference at
the granularities we've tested.

## What Phase 27 will tell us

Phase 27 swaps the keyword classifier for the LLM-based one (the
Phase 19 mechanism applied to user prompts instead of system
prompts). Hypothesis: if classifier *accuracy* was the issue, P27
should show a meaningful lift over P26's 40%. If filtering itself
is the issue, P27 will be approximately the same.

My prediction: P27 will be roughly the same. The Phase 26 result
strongly suggests filtering-not-accuracy is the cause.

## Verdict

**CLARIFYING NEGATIVE — content routing should NOT be enabled.**
The Phase 21 hint is confirmed at proper statistical power.
The knob ships but defaults off; future research could revisit
with a diversity-preserving variant (e.g. "70% EC + 30% myth"
weighted sampling instead of binary filter).
